## Krathwohl (2002)

Krathwohl, D. R. (2002). A revision of Bloom's taxonomy: An overview. *Theory Into Practice, 41*(4), 212-218. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15430421tip4104_2

Read: full text.

- A learning objective pairs a content noun with a cognitive verb. The revision splits these into a Knowledge dimension (factual, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive) and a Cognitive Process dimension (remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create).
- Analyses of objectives and test items have repeatedly found heavy emphasis on recall.
- The Taxonomy Table (knowledge type by cognitive process) classifies objectives, activities and assessments. Its blank cells show missed teaching opportunities.
- The hierarchy is relaxed. Categories overlap, and some Understand processes are more complex than some Apply processes.

Used in: "Write the test" steps 1 and 2. The skill uses the lesson `kind` values for rows in place of the four knowledge types.

## Kang, McDermott and Roediger (2007)

Kang, S. H. K., McDermott, K. B., & Roediger, H. L. (2007). Test format and corrective feedback modify the effect of testing on long-term retention. *European Journal of Cognitive Psychology, 19*(4/5), 528-558. https://doi.org/10.1080/09541440601056620

Read: full text.

- College students read short journal papers, took a multiple choice (MC) quiz, a short answer (SA) quiz, read statements of the facts, or did a filler task, then took a final test three days later.
- Without feedback, MC led to better final performance than SA, but did not differ significantly from reading the statements. Initial scores were 86% on MC and 54% on SA.
- With feedback after each item, SA gave the best final performance. MC did not differ significantly from reading the statements.
- After a wrong MC answer, students endorsed the same wrong option on the final MC test with probability .65 without feedback and .30 with feedback.
- SA responses were scored 1, one half, or 0.
- Feedback arrived right after each item. The effect of delayed feedback needs further research.

Used in: "Write the test" steps 3 and 4, and "Mark and review".

## Roediger and Karpicke (2006)

Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science, 17*(3), 249-255. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x

Read: abstract only.

- Students studied prose passages, then took one or three immediate free-recall tests without feedback, or restudied the same number of times.
- Final test after 5 minutes, 2 days or 1 week. At 5 minutes, restudy gave better recall. At 2 days and 1 week, prior testing gave substantially better retention, even though restudy raised confidence.

Used in: the retest.

## Haladyna, Downing and Rodriguez (2002)

Haladyna, T. M., Downing, S. M., & Rodriguez, M. C. (2002). A review of multiple-choice item-writing guidelines for classroom assessment. *Applied Measurement in Education, 15*(3), 309-333.

Read: abstract only.

- A taxonomy of 31 multiple-choice item-writing guidelines, validated against 27 educational testing textbooks and 27 research studies published since 1990.

For multiple-choice item rules, point to this paper. The skill doesn't restate its guidelines.

## Black and Wiliam (1998)

Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (1998). Assessment and classroom learning. *Assessment in Education: Principles, Policy & Practice, 5*(1), 7-74.

Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (1998, October). Inside the black box: Raising standards through classroom assessment. *Phi Delta Kappan.*

- Studies of improved formative assessment show significant and often substantial learning gains.
